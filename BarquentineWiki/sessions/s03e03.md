# Is This Where Sand Comes From?

*S3E3 · recorded 2026-09-09 · source: `GMT20260909-011950_Recording.transcript.vtt`* · recap: DM's S3e4 intro (Google Doc "Barquentine S3 notes")

The crew found Mira waiting on a dead world — an old woman who's kept a dying stranger's promise to protect this place for more years than she can count, and who's been alone with it ever since. She walked them out to the gully where the tremors start, and the planet answered on cue: a hole tore open in the glass surface, twenty feet of hide breaching like a whale from the ocean, and gone again before anyone could finish flinching. Up close, the damage told its own story — glass gone from shattered to granular to smooth, radiating out from the borehole like a wound. Room took to the air to scout the tunnels from above, and the worm passed directly beneath him — close enough that Lumi felt something in his own chest answer back, unbidden, a cleric's oath recognizing the shape of death – and life –  passing underneath. Not evil. Not good. Just old. Medicinal.

Elder Abarat, walking his ruined homeworld for the first time in two hundred years, asked Mira the only question that mattered to him: had she found any trace of his people? She had — pieces she'd never been able to place. A statue, ten to fifteen feet tall, half-buried and half-remembered: one humanoid figure standing beside one automaton. The first of his kind, the one who turned himself and everyone he loved into Warforged rather than let his people die on this planet.

Back at camp, the argument the crew had been circling came out in the open: do they kill this thing, or does something else have to change? Mira wants it dead — it's eating the only home she has left, one bite at a time. Lumi isn't so sure, and he went looking for an answer the hard way: shut himself in with incense and candlelight and cast Legend Lore on the worm itself, only to have the spell redirect — the worm isn't a person, isn't a place, and isn't an object, it's a symptom. What came back was a vision about death and balance, about a world that's been scarred and a cycle looking for a way to complete itself, Death, the vision told him, isn't good or evil — it's scientific, procedural. Lumi, suspecting the thing under the glass has better bedside manner than he does, went to bed turning it over and got back up, unable to let it go: *"They're not gonna kill this thing."*

Room's map had one more thing to say. As the crew's next destination would have blinked into view — the way it's done before, one point after another — it didn't. Still this planet. Still the worm, the glass, and Mira's two hundred years of waiting. Can the crew decide what to do about the worm without knowing what "wrong" means? What does a cleric do with a diagnosis he can't fully translate? And what's still down here that the map won't let them leave behind?

**Find out this time, on Barquentine!**

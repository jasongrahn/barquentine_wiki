# Chewing Mold

*S2E13 · recorded date unknown · source: `Barquentine! S2E13 Chewing Mold.transcript.vtt`*

First snow of the year, and a ship on the horizon. No crew on deck, no wake, nothing but drift. The crew of the Manticore circle it carefully, broadside, close enough to board... and the trap springs. Tentacles rise. It's no ship at all but a mimic the size of one, and it glues itself to their hull and starts tearing planks. Basil yells its name: Lucy. Room, who hates mimics more than open water, slaps a magic feather on it that only works on real boats. Cyan tries to talk it down and gets nowhere. In the end it's Lumi's spiritual weapon, a gigantic gyoza, that dives into the beast's crown and boils it from the top down. As Lucy sinks, Lumi sums it up: *"that's the way the noodle rumbles."* Below decks, Ted plugs a leak with chewing mold.

On the way to Bral, Ted spins tales of the Veil of Mist, a living fog that judges every ship that passes and decides its fate. The whole crew put their souls on the scale. Room fails. Ted fails worse. The Veil lets them through anyway. Alarms ring out across the harbor, but the crowd on shore isn't a war party: half of Bral is stoked to see a skull-faced ship pull in, and Cletus is in the crowd. In port sits a cherry-wood ship with a giant cheeseburger on its mainsail.

Under the Elven Rodeo, a nightclub full of identical elves, the DJ drops a cage on Room and summons a spectral unicorn for him to ride. He's thrown, and the crowd laughs. In the back office, Mr. Catastrophe makes good on the deal. The ship is one of the most vile artifacts on the planet, but it can be redeemed, at a toll on everyone who crews it: *"a ship is only as good as its crew."* Every loxodon knows the way home, he tells Room, and with linked magic from 200 elves he can send the crew into Room's dreams to dig the map out. Then Cyan names Hierophant Vermilion. Cyan is sure of it now: Vermilion is his brother. And Mr. Catastrophe has a summons from a religious cloister, asking for hands to deliver goods, from a serious-sounding man who just might be Cyan's kin. He can gate them over in the morning.

Lumi asks a careful hypothetical about a bronzed torso locked in a chest in the celestial plane. Mr. Catastrophe's guess: someone made a deal with a god, and the chest should stay locked. And Ted comes back chipper, restored, with an invitation from a talk show. What's waiting at the cloister? What does Vermilion want with his brother? And who books a crew like this for television? Find out this time, on Barquentine!

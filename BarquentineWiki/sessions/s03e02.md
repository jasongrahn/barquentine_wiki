# Job's Done!

*S3E2 · recorded 2026-08-26 · source: `GMT20260826-005401_Recording.transcript.vtt`*

Room dreamed he was wearing someone else. Flat, colorless ground, somehow warm. Two suns. Something falling from the sky in a spread that never ended. The person he was wearing knew exactly what was coming, and wasn't running. At the edge of the dream waited a shapeless thing with eyes that opened and closed like bubbles in oil. So Room said what his grandmother taught him: *"Baku come eat my dreams."* The answer came faster than it ever had. It didn't eat the dream. It ate the ground. And in the half second before he woke, Room understood what he'd been standing on: someone's last memory.

That same night, Elder Abarath came to the Captain with a speech he had clearly practiced. Before they landed, he wanted the Captain to know what his world had been like. Rings where the ordnance struck. An ocean you can no longer find. And the creature: no shape that stayed, hundreds of eyes, moving through buildings the way water moves through cloth. When it passed, the buildings were still standing, and the people inside were simply gone. One of his people went to the gods and paid with everything he was, so the rest could leave. If the creature survived the glassing, Abarath said, they are all doomed.

Then the marble filled the sky. It was a perfect bead between two suns, so bright and smooth that the crew watched their own ship falling toward them. A world like that needs legs to land on, and the ship named its price: Lumi's dead Giff general, soul coin and all. The deck drew back like lips from teeth, folded the body under, and pushed out four knuckled legs of bone, tiny under that hull, like a beetle's. Inside the Captain's head, the ship crowed: *"Job's done!"* Alistair and Victoria watched in silence, then walked away. The Captain gave a standing order: from now on, the crew collects undesirables.

Four perfect footfalls on glass with nothing to grip. The Manticore slid and slid until it hit rough ground, tore off its legs, and fell on its side. Before the crew had finished climbing down, an old woman came running across the glass. Her name was Mira. She'd been stranded for years, keeping a dying stranger's promise to protect this place. Something was eating her world: a worm, hundreds of feet long, tunneling through solid glass. Help her kill it, she said, and everything she's found is theirs.

Is the creature from Room's dream still buried here? What does Mira really want from a crew with a hungry ship? And how many undesirables will it take to put the Manticore back on its feet? Find out this time, on Barquentine!

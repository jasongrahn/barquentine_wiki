# Job's Done!

*S3E2 · recorded 2026-08-26 · source: `GMT20260826-005401_Recording.transcript.vtt`* · recap: DM's S3e3 intro (Google Doc "Barquentine S3 notes")

A large barge market gave the crew a rare peaceful afternoon — Room walked off with a portable hole and a hat of disguise, Lumi traded a dream-stitched cloak for a coin humming with somebody else's rage, and the Captain bought himself an eye patch to match the eye he lost springing his parents from brig. Then the ship named its price for legs to land on a dead world: not wood, not coin, a body. Alistair and Victoria watched their son feed a month-old corpse into the deck of a living ship and didn't pretend to approve. Four legs grew down, wrong and hungry. Insatiable, the standing order went out anyway: from now on, the crew collects "undesirables"

Before landing, Elder Abarat finally told the Captain what happened to his world — not just a war, but a faceless, hundred-eyed shape that moved through walls like water through cloth, leaving buildings standing and the people inside consumed - not killed - used. One of his people bartered with the gods to fight it, paying with everything he was instead of his life — the only reason Abarat can walk - and fear -  at all today. Nobody knows if the thing survived. Room had already dreamed it that same night: a stranger's body, two suns, things falling from the sky without end, and his grandmother's old trick against nightmares answering faster and hungrier than ever before — not eating the dream this time, but the ground itself. The ground, it turned out, was somebody's last memory.

The marble looked back at the crew like a mirror until they saw the black lines wandering under its glass, curving the way real cracks never do. The landing went about as well as four brand-new legs on a dead flat sheet of glass could: the Manticore skidded, sheared off the front legs, cracked, and went down hard on its side. Alistair and Victoria stayed with the wreck — "so that if you all died, that many less people would be dead" — while Elder Abarat led the way onto his ruined homeworld for the first time in two hundred years. There they met Mira, stranded for years and keeping a dying stranger's promise to protect this place, who told them the truth: it isn't a memory or a god eating this planet, it's a worm, hundreds of feet of it and still growing, tunneling through solid glass. Help me kill it, she said, and what treasure I found is yours.

Can the crew put down something that's been growing since before any of them were born? What will they learn from their worm-bound reconnaissance? What's actually buried under the ruins? Find out this time, on Barquentine!
